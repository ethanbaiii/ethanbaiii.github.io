I began with a Rubik's Cube-inspired rotation mechanic, aiming for complex puzzles players could solve through step-by-step reasoning. Designing those levels proved difficult, and the final layout was largely linear.

<h4 id="spatial-flip-puzzle-findings-reflection">Findings &amp; Reflection</h4>

During playtesting, the number of buttons, available operations, and spatial changes left players disoriented. They began trying inputs at random rather than identifying patterns and working through a solution as intended.

The level was solvable, but the experience exposed a gap between solvability and readability. Tracking the changing space interfered with the intended reasoning. Testing a spatial puzzle needs to examine whether players can understand what an action changed and use that understanding to plan their next move, alongside checking that a solution exists.
