<h4 id="tower-trade-off-findings-reflection">Findings &amp; Reflection</h4>

In my playtesting, random placement was more punishing: short-range towers could land beyond reach of the path, making success heavily dependent on luck. Repeatedly choosing the longest-range tower had a clear advantage. Randomizing tower types while keeping placement control was easier.

This exposed an imbalance between the sacrifices. Placement could determine whether a tower contributed at all, making long range the safest response to uncertainty. The experiment showed how removing one form of control can also narrow the choices left behind: a meaningful sacrifice needs room for adaptation, rather than making options ineffective.
