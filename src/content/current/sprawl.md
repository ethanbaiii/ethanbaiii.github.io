## Gameplay: Expand, Cut Off, and Survive

Action cards determine the size of vine tile players may place. Players choose a shape and orientation, then connect it to an open growing tip of their vine. Placement can extend a route, create branches, or cover opponents' vines.

Growing tips are both expansion points and vulnerable targets. Covering an opponent's tip removes that growth option and earns bonus points; losing every tip eliminates a player. Visible territory scores at the end, while the last surviving vine wins immediately. Players therefore weigh expanding their area against cutting off rivals and preserving their own tips. Special cards can protect a board space or grant an additional action on the next turn.

## Iteration: Creating Conflict and Preserving Planning

Early tests allowed too much independent expansion. We reduced the initial 13×13 board to 9×9, introduced elimination through blocked tips, and rewarded covering opponents' tips to encourage direct competition.

Initially, players drew a random shape after an action card determined its size. This could invalidate a planned move. Allowing shape selection removed that second layer of randomness while retaining the action-card constraint.

## Adapting to a Classroom Time Limit

We were satisfied with the territory-control experience at around 40–60 minutes, but the assignment required under 20. Further board reductions to 7×7, fewer rounds, and additional opening placements aimed to compress the experience.

Later reports still included a 45-minute session. Players enjoyed attacking and anticipating others, but overlapping vines also made connections and growing tips difficult to track. Terminology, diagrams, and indicators were revised; meeting the classroom limit and making the board readable remained separate challenges.
