## Gameplay: Expand, Cut Off, and Survive

Action cards determine the size of vine tile players may place. Players choose a shape and orientation, then connect it to an open growing tip of their vine. Placement can extend a route, create branches, or cover opponents' vines.

Growing tips are both expansion points and vulnerable targets. Covering an opponent's tip removes that growth option and earns bonus points. Losing every tip eliminates a player. Visible territory scores at the end, while the last surviving vine wins immediately. Players therefore weigh expanding their area against cutting off rivals and preserving their own tips. In addition, item cards can protect a board space or grant an additional action on the next turn.

## Iteration: Creating Conflict and Preserving Planning

Early tests allowed too much independent expansion. We reduced the initial 13×13 board to 9×9, introduced elimination through blocked tips, and rewarded covering opponents' tips to encourage direct competition.

Initially, players drew a random shape after an action card determined its size. This could invalidate a planned move. Allowing shape selection removed that second layer of randomness while retaining the action-card constraint.

## Reflection

We were satisfied with the territory-control experience at around 40–60 minutes, but the assignment required under 20. Further board reductions to 7×7, fewer rounds, and additional opening placements aimed to compress the experience. However, a later report still recorded a 45-minute session.

This experience showed me that play time depends on more than board size and round count. The metagame takes time: players think through moves, talk, and try to read one another. Keeping these interactions while fitting a session into 20 minutes was a challenge for us.

Players enjoyed attacking one another and trying to anticipate each other's next move. However, overlapping vines made it hard to see which sections were connected and which tips could still grow. We revised the rule wording, diagrams, and indicators to help players read the board more easily.
