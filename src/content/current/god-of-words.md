## Gameplay: Bid, Build an Answer, and Persuade

Players share a theme and receive a limited budget. Each auction reveals letters, and players secretly bid for picking priority. Everyone participating pays their own bid. Higher bids choose first. Players weigh securing a useful letter against saving money for later, and can skip unwanted auctions. Dedicated vowel auctions offer opportunities to complete an idea.

The collected letters become the material for a themed answer. Players do not need to use every letter: words, phrases, abbreviations, and non-English answers are allowed if the group accepts their connection to the theme. Everyone reveals and explains their answer, then secretly votes for another player's response. The player with the most votes wins.

This connects two forms of play: planning which letters to pursue and improvising an interpretation from the letters obtained.

## Iteration: More Choice Within the Constraint

Early feedback showed that spending more did not reliably secure a desired letter. We changed the auction to ranked picking, replaced later random vowel draws with vowel auctions, and allowed players to skip rather than pay for unwanted letters.

The supply of letters remained limited, and we did not add trading at the end of the game. This preserved the trade-offs in bidding.

## Adjusting the Auction Phase

The ending shifted from players exhausting their budgets to a round limit. Initially, the round limit matched the number of players, but four rounds felt too short in a four-player game, so we increased it. However, six-player tests with nine rounds outlasted some players' interest once they had an answer ready. We settled on six rounds before the reveal and voting.
